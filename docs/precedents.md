# Precedent presets: what makes a tall mixed-use plan work

"Start from a precedent" (Presets button, Verdict tab and Ask AI) maps the stack of a built or approved tower onto your floors:
- by height fraction, with your geometry unchanged;
- level 1 keeps retail and a lobby, and below grade is parking;
- locked floors stay;
- plant floors go about every 20 floors when the precedent lists none;
- one Undo reverses it.

Each preset says what it relied on. It can turn on its own market context as a clearly labelled scenario, which never changes the 2026 defaults by itself. The data is in `catalogue/precedents.json`; floor ranges are approximate where sources differ, and each preset says how.

Research: public summaries read on 18 October 2026 (`checked: summary`). Oakridge Park is left out: no reliable floor-by-floor data was found.

## What the precedents have in common

1. **Most did not buy land at today's price in today's market.** Church land, owner-held land and ground leases took land cost out, and strong presale markets (mid-2000s Vancouver and Toronto, 2015–19) carried the condos.
2. **Height is earned by homes or a landmark.** Luxury condos near the top, or a deck that is valued as an asset in itself (Edge), pay for the tall-building premium. Offices high up rarely do, unless pre-leased to an anchor.
3. **Hotels sit low** (the first 15–17 floors) as a brand and amenity base. They seldom carry the tower alone (Bay Parkade relies on its condo towers).
4. **Public and civic space is delivered in kind,** paid for by the market floors: the church, daycare, social homes and community centre. A separate building or a City partner often takes it.
5. **None of the Vancouver precedents meets a 9.0 FSR non-residential rule.** They are mainly residential. Those that come close (Lotte, Rainier) do so with offices that need an anchor tenant or owner-held land.

**How to read it:**
- Under 2026 numbers, the residential precedents reach "Marginal": money left over, but a weak presale market caps the verdict.
- With their own context (presales working, or no land purchase), several reach "Works".
- None meets the brief's 9.0 FSR non-residential rule. On this site the rule is the main obstacle, not the stacking.
- Plans built on offices or hotels fail on money unless the land is free or an anchor pre-leases the space.

Market scenarios in `assumptions.json` `scenarios`:
- **2026 now:** the default.
- **Strong market (about 2021):** presales working; rental values ×1.5 from Q4 2021 cap rates of 2.00–2.75% (grade B); hard costs −12% (grade C).
- **Recovery (about 2028):** all grade C assumptions.

Each one is an optional overlay shown in the header pill.
